Sources and limits.

Independent screening project

The flood grid is still about 90 m.

The blue footprint comes from KSDMA’s historical flood model. Small drains, narrow channels and local terrain can be missed. Enlarging it on screen does not create new detail.

More context is not a new model.

The 30 m Copernicus surface model helps show relief. OSM adds 8,364 mapped waterway segments. Neither changes the flood footprint or proves that the model is correct.

An overlap is a place to look closer.

A school symbol is a representative point for a mapped record; the overlap test used its mapped geometry. Road crossings do not prove a bridge will flood or a route will close.

Read the record review below. Source download dates are not proof that every record is current. Most infrastructure records have not been independently checked.

Where the data comes from

Flood footprints. Kerala State Disaster Management Authority, historical 10-, 25- and 50-year scenarios. The source model and its grid are unchanged.

Schools, health records, major roads, places and waterways. © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL. These are mapped records, not a complete or current official inventory. Health types follow OSM tags and can include hospitals, clinics, centres and medical practices. This is not a verified or complete health-service register.

Surface relief. Copernicus GLO-30, a digital surface model, not a bare-earth drainage survey. Produced using Copernicus WorldDEM-30 © DLR e.V. 2010–2014 and © Airbus Defence and Space GmbH 2014–2018, provided under COPERNICUS by the European Union and ESA; all rights reserved.

District boundary. Open Data Kerala district boundary. Local-body boundaries use a separate community snapshot, with checks described below.

Map library and type. Leaflet and Public Sans. The page uses plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript.
